## Tier 1 — Apply first, highest leverage

### 1. NORAD — Tax for Development / Oil for Development programmes
- **Source:** Norwegian Agency for Development Cooperation
- **Programme budget pool:** ~USD 80M+ annually across the two programmes
- **Realistic ask for RC:** **USD 500K – 1.5M** as a 2–3 year programme grant
- **Why it fits:** Norway is the single largest national funder of EITI globally. Their Tax for Development line funds revenue-mobilization technology in resource-rich African states. RC is the textbook match.
- **Application window:** Annual cycle, typically October submission for following year. Bilateral consultations with NORAD Lusaka or Oslo can happen any time.
- **What to lead with:** ZEITI engagement letter (when we get one) + ToB SOW + ZRA contract intent. Norway respects institutional ladder; lead with credentials.
- **Difficulty:** Medium-high. Bureaucratic, but predictable if you have the right institutional cover.
- **Status:** Not yet contacted. **First step: identify NORAD's Lusaka office contact.**

### 2. AfDB — Digital Public Infrastructure / Governance window
- **Source:** African Development Bank
- **Relevant facilities:** Africa Digital Financial Inclusion Facility (ADFI), African Legal Support Facility (ALSF), Governance Trust Fund
- **Realistic ask for RC:** **USD 300K – 1M** as a digital governance pilot or sovereign-tax-modernisation grant
- **Why it fits:** AfDB is already a validator node in the RC demo. The Clean Cooking program launch (per intel feed) signals AfDB is in active deploy mode. African revenue mobilization is on-mandate.
- **Application window:** Rolling for facility-level grants; specific calls quarterly.
- **What to lead with:** Validator-node demo + AfDB intel feed showing Zambia/extractives focus. The pre-existing institutional relationship via the demo panel is the warm-up.
- **Difficulty:** Medium. AfDB is institutional but African-led, friendlier reception for African applicants.
- **Status:** Validator node in demo; no formal application yet.

### 3. EU Horizon Europe — Critical Raw Materials Act / Battery Passport calls
- **Source:** European Commission, DG GROW / DG RTD
- **Relevant calls:** Critical Raw Materials Act implementation calls, Battery Passport pilot calls, Horizon CL4 (Digital, Industry, Space)
- **Realistic ask for RC:** **EUR 500K – 2M (USD 550K – 2.2M)** as a pilot project, typically part of a small consortium
- **Why it fits:** RC is structurally what the CRMA and Battery Passport regulation require. Source-layer cryptographic attestation is the regulatory mandate.
- **Application window:** Specific calls open for 4–6 months. Multiple battery-related calls open in 2026.
- **Consortium requirement:** Usually need 1+ EU partner (automotive OEM, EU research institute, EU mining company). KoBold has US/UK presence — not enough alone. Need a Fraunhofer / KU Leuven / TUM type partner.
- **What to lead with:** ToB SOW + the cryptographic verification primitive + ZEITI methodology validation
- **Difficulty:** High. EU paperwork is brutal; consortium-building is slow. But the money is real and the regulation is the forcing function.
- **Status:** Not yet contacted. **Identify EU consortium partner needed first.**

## Strategic note — why grants > VC at this stage

You have a sovereign-infrastructure product. VC money carries equity dilution AND signalling problems:

- **VC signal to ZRA:** "this is a startup looking for an exit." Bad.
- **Grant signal to ZRA:** "this is a sovereign-aligned project backed by AfDB / NORAD / EU." Good.
- **VC signal to ZEITI:** "they have investors with return expectations." Suspicious.
- **Grant signal to ZEITI:** "this is institutionally funded, mission-aligned." Welcome.

Take VC later, post-pilot, when the product is de-risked and the institutional foundation is built. **Right now, every dollar of grant money is worth ~3 dollars of VC money** because of the credibility multiplier with your actual buyers.
