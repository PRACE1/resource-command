# ZEITI Call Prep — Plain Language Edition

**For Kennedy.** This is the version you read the morning of the call and the night before.
**Ian is institutional, not technical.** Talk to him like a senior policy analyst, not an engineer.

---

## The frame to walk in with

Ian doesn't care about cryptography. He cares about:

- **Methodology** — does this fit how ZEITI already works?
- **Independence** — does this keep ZEITI neutral?
- **Reputation** — what does this do for ZEITI's standing inside Zambia and globally?
- **His career** — could this be the thing that makes him known beyond Lusaka?

Talk to those four things. Everything else is noise.

## How to explain what RC is (in 30 seconds)

**The sealed envelope.** This is the one metaphor that does all the work.

> "Imagine a sealed envelope. The mining company puts their actual production numbers inside — tons of copper, the grade, the price. The envelope produces a stamp on the outside that says *'the royalty calculation based on these numbers is correct.'* The government — or ZEITI — can check the stamp and know it's valid. But the envelope stays sealed. Nobody ever sees the numbers inside.
>
> Right now ZEITI has to trust what mining companies put in their reports. With this, the report itself comes with a built-in proof that the math is right. Same ZEITI methodology — just with a lie detector baked in."

That's the whole pitch. If you say nothing else technical, that's enough.

**Do not say:** zero-knowledge proof, cryptographic protocol, R1CS constraints, BN254, Groth16, Circom, hash commitment, BFT consensus.
**Do say:** sealed envelope, stamp, verification, math that can't be faked, lie detector, trust without exposure.

## What he'll likely ask, and how to answer (plain words)

### "What is this technology, in simple terms?"
> "It's a way for someone to prove a calculation is correct without showing you the inputs. So a mining company can prove their royalty payment is right, without handing over their production volumes, grades, or contract prices. The math behind it is settled — it's been used for years in cryptocurrency. We're applying it to royalty compliance."

### "How does ZEITI's role change?"
> "It doesn't. ZEITI still reconciles. ZEITI still publishes. ZEITI still convenes the multi-stakeholder group. What changes is that the numbers ZEITI publishes can be mathematically reproduced by any auditor without ZEITI re-exposing the operator's data. So when a journalist or civil society group challenges a number, ZEITI's answer becomes 'here's the math' instead of 'here's our process.'"

### "Why would mining companies agree to this?"
> "Two reasons. One — they keep their commercial confidentiality. Right now, when they report to ZEITI, their production figures eventually become visible. With this, the figures stay private but the compliance is provable. Two — they need this for the EU Battery Passport anyway. From February 2027, every kilogram of cobalt or copper into Europe needs cryptographic attestation. If RC handles ZEITI and the EU at the same time, that's two compliance regimes for the cost of one. They save money."

### "Who's reviewing this?"
> "Two parallel reviews. Trail of Bits — they're the people who audited the major American cryptocurrency systems. They have the technical pack and we're in formal scoping. Independently, Barry Whitehat — he's one of the foundational figures in this kind of cryptography, kind of like a Vint Cerf for this space — has the same pack and has been stress-testing it for weeks. Both reviews are running at the same time. The technical credibility is being built in parallel with this institutional conversation, not after."

### "What does this cost ZEITI?"
> "Nothing direct. ZEITI's role is methodological — validator, not customer. There's no budget ask. The commercial side is between RC and the Zambian operating entity that contracts with ZRA. ZEITI just lends its methodological credibility, and in exchange, ZEITI gets to be the chapter that authored the next big methodology in transparency reporting."

### "How long does a pilot take?"
> "Ninety days. The first month is mapping ZEITI's existing methodology onto what RC does — making sure nothing is lost in translation. The second month we run RC against three years of past ZEITI reconciliation data and prove we get the same numbers. The third month we run a live pilot on one mine. End of 90 days, you have a methodology note from the ZEITI Secretariat that says 'this works.'"

### "What happens after the pilot?"
> "That methodology note travels. First to Oslo, to the EITI International Secretariat. Then to other chapters facing the same pressures — DRC for cobalt, Tanzania for gold, Indonesia for nickel. Within two years, the goal is for this to be part of the next EITI International Standard revision. Zambia goes from being the chapter that adopted the methodology, to the chapter that wrote it."

### "Why hasn't anyone else done this?"
> "Two reasons. The technology only really became practical in the last two years — the math has been around since 2016, but the engineering caught up recently. And the political will has only just arrived. Konkola, the EU regulation, ZRA's tax modernisation, the election cycle — those are converging now in Zambia in a way they're not converging anywhere else. Norway has the cryptography talent but not the urgency. Indonesia has the urgency but not the institutional independence. Zambia has both right now, in the same window."

### "Who else have you spoken to in government?"
> "I've had one positive conversation with David Wamulume at the Ministry of Energy — he was supportive but flagged that mining isn't his mandate. He offered to introduce me to the right ministry, which is what I'm working through. I haven't approached ZRA directly yet because I wanted to have ZEITI's methodological view first. If the methodology is sound from ZEITI's perspective, the ZRA conversation gets dramatically easier."

*That last line matters. It signals you respect ZEITI's role enough to come to them BEFORE going to ZRA, not after. That's flattering without being obvious about it.*

## The four asks at the end — plain words

Read his energy. If he's leaning in after #2, push to #4 the same breath. If he's measured, land #3 cleanly and let #4 come up naturally later.

### Ask 1 — Does this make sense to you?
> "From what we've walked through — does this sound like it fits what the G-Factor framework was designed to do? Or are there gaps I need to be honest about?"

*This is foundational. If he says yes, everything else follows. If he flags gaps, you've just been given the most valuable information of the call — use it.*

### Ask 2 — Can we test it on real data?
> "If the alignment is there, the next step is to prove it. Could ZEITI Secretariat share methodology specs and three recent reconciliation cycles, suitably anonymised, so we can show the math reproduces the same totals? That turns the conversation from theoretical to evidenced."

*Concrete, modest, technical-sounding without being technical. He can say yes to this without committing to anything political.*

### Ask 3 — Can we present this to your stakeholders together?
> "Once the replay lands, would there be a route to present it to the right ZEITI audience? Multi-Stakeholder Group, Secretariat technical session, whichever fits. Having the three constituencies — government, industry, civil society — hear it at the same time is the fastest path to consensus that this is a Zambian deliverable, not an outside one."

*This makes Ian look like a convener inside ZEITI, not like he's championing an outside vendor. That's important to his standing.*

### Ask 4 — The University of Zambia introduction (NEW)
> "And there's a closer one that would matter immediately — would you be open to introducing us to the right team at the University of Zambia? We're thinking of UNZA as a local academic research partner — strengthens the methodology work for grant applications and gives us Zambian academic credibility alongside ZRA. Particularly Economics or the School of Mines, depending on the angle. We'd be looking at a consortium relationship for grant funding, not just a courtesy chat."

*UNZA before Oslo is intentional. UNZA is a smaller political stake for Ian (he likely has direct counterparts there — they probably sit on the ZEITI Multistakeholder Group). A "yes" here warms him toward the bigger Oslo "yes" that follows. Behavioural escalation, not pressure.*

**If he asks "which UNZA unit?":**

> "UNZA Economics would be the strongest fit — they already work with ZRA and have UNU-WIDER partnerships (which is the team behind the tax-gap research we cite). School of Mines is the alternative if you'd rather route it through the mining sector academic side. Happy to defer to whoever you know best."

*UNZA Economics is the right answer — they train ZRA economists, they author the tax-gap research, they have donor familiarity (NORAD, AfDB, Hewlett, IDRC). But always give Ian latitude on the department — he'll route it to whoever he actually has the relationship with.*

### Ask 5 — The big one. Will you introduce me to Oslo?
> "And looking further out — if Zambia becomes the first chapter to pair the methodology with this verification layer, the natural next conversation is at the global Secretariat level in Oslo. You're uniquely placed to be that bridge. Would you be open to making an introduction when the time is right?"

*"When the time is right" is the load-bearing phrase. It gives him a graceful park. He doesn't have to commit on the call. He can think about it, talk to his Oslo counterparts informally, and offer the introduction when he's ready. Pushing for immediate action on this closes the door. Patience opens it.*

*Coming after the UNZA ask, this lands better than as the standalone fourth ask. He's already said yes to introducing you locally — saying yes to introducing you globally is a smaller psychological step than it would have been cold.*

## Things to avoid saying

- **"Disruptive."** This word terrifies institutional people. ZEITI is institutional. We are not disrupting ZEITI. We are completing ZEITI's methodology.
- **"Replace."** RC doesn't replace anything. It adds a verification layer.
- **"Blockchain."** Even if Ian uses it, you don't. The word has too much baggage. Use "cryptographic verification" or "mathematical proof."
- **"Smart contract."** Same — too crypto-bro.
- **"We're a startup."** You're an IP holding company with a sovereign mandate. Sound like it.
- **"Revolution."** Avoid all evangelical language. ZEITI is a patient, institutional body.
- **Specific operator names** unless he raises them first. Don't name First Quantum or Vedanta or KoBold unprompted — keeps you politically clean.
- **The $940M figure.** Use the range *"$550M to $940M."* The headline figure alone sounds activist; the range sounds analytical.

## Things to lean into

- **"Methodology."** This is the word that anchors his world. Use it often.
- **"Reconciliation."** ZEITI's bread and butter. Reference it.
- **"Multi-stakeholder."** His governance vocabulary.
- **"Independence."** What ZEITI lives or dies by. Reassure constantly.
- **"Chapter Lead."** This is the role you're inviting him into. Don't say it directly — let the brief plant it. But hold the frame in your head.
- **"The next Standard revision."** This is the EITI prize. Mention it once. Don't overdo.
- **"Verifiable" / "reproducible" / "auditable."** These are his words. Use his words.

## If something goes wrong

### He says "I need to escalate to the Secretariat before responding."
> "Of course. That's the right process. Is there anything I can put together that would make the internal conversation easier — a one-page summary for the Secretariat, a methodology comparison document, anything that travels well internally?"

*You just turned an escalation into a follow-up task. That's good.*

### He says "This sounds too sophisticated for our current capacity."
> "I hear that. The pilot is structured so ZEITI doesn't have to build any of the technology — that's on our side. ZEITI's role is methodological review and validation. The capacity ask is the same as the capacity ZEITI already deploys for reconciliation work."

*Reframe from "you have to build something" to "you do the thing you already do, we do the rest."*

### He says "The minister will want to hear about this."
> "That's a great signal. I'd be grateful for any opportunity to brief them — but I want to be respectful of ZEITI's relationship with the ministry. What's the right way for that conversation to happen?"

*Receive it positively. Don't push for the minister intro. Let HIM offer it.*

### He goes quiet.
**You go quiet too.** Don't fill the silence with more pitch. ZEITI people think before they speak. Give him 5–10 seconds. The instinct will be to say more — resist. The next thing he says after a silence is usually the most important thing in the call.
